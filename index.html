          Why passive

          Elders don't wear devices. Homes can still collect the data.

          Watches and rings need charging, pairing and daily habit, and adherence drops within weeks. Dhyaan's sensors sit where life already happens: the bathroom floor, the bed, the doorway.

          ~100M

          adults in India living with diabetes, all at risk of foot ulcers and amputation.

          ~150M

          Indians aged 60+, a number set to roughly double by 2050.

          Weeks

          of warning before a foot ulcer appears: inflammation shows as one foot running hotter than the other.

          Where the AI lives

          One sensor gives readings. Three give a person.

          The model learns each elder's normal and flags change across signals, combining them into one plain-language alert instead of five noisy ones.

          Personal baselines

          “Normal” is Lakshmi's normal, not a population average. A resting heart rate of 76 matters if hers is usually 68.

          Cross-signal patterns

          More bed exits + worse sleep + rising sway = fall risk rising, even if each signal alone looks borderline.

          Confirm before alerting

          Multi-day confirmation keeps false alarms down, so families and doctors keep trusting the alerts.

          Business model

          Hardware near cost. Monitoring by subscription.

          The sensors are the way in; the ongoing value is the monitoring, the alerts and the doctor loop. That is what people pay for every month.

          Who pays first

          Families who want early warning for a parent living alone, especially those living in another city or abroad.

          How we reach them

          Diabetologists recommend the mat to patients at risk of foot ulcers; word of mouth carries it from there.

          Start with one device

          The foot-health mat solves one urgent, costly problem and proves people will pay.

          Expand around the home

          Bed and motion sensors join the same subscription, turning one alert into a full picture of the person.

          The moat is the data

          Years of in-home signals per elder make the AI sharper and the doctor relationship stickier over time.
